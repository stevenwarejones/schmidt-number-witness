# Independent review

Reviewer: Claude (Anthropic), 6 September 2026. Adversarial review of the certificates in
this tree. **This is independent AI review, not human expert review and not peer review.** Checks
labelled numerical are floating-point and finite-sample; the exact claims rest on the
repository's symbolic verifiers.
Every result below was recomputed with separately written code; none of it is a rerun of the
repository's own verifiers, though those were also executed and pass.

## What the pre-certificate numerical attack showed

Before the Gram certificate existed, an earlier snapshot of this tree stated the qubit bound
as a conjecture. A numerical attack on it, shipped in this directory, found:

- `max_U G(theta)` decreasing across a 26-point grid, with optimizer values at the endpoints
  agreeing with 7 and 6 to solver precision. A sampled decreasing sequence is not a proof of
  global monotonicity of the optimized envelope, and the endpoint figures are optimizer
  results, not exact evaluations. Shipped workload per grid point:
  **880,000 sampled configurations** (12 batches of 40,000, plus 400,000 more drawn to seed
  the polish step) followed by 12 Nelder-Mead polishes — `research/qubit_theta_profile.py`;
- **600 Nelder-Mead restarts** jointly over `(theta, U)` returning `7.000000000000002`,
  attained at `theta = 0` — `research/qubit_joint_search.py`. The search clips theta to `[0, pi/4]`,
  which creates flat regions outside the physical interval; that is a search-efficiency
  artifact and this is not certified global optimization;
- a deficit consistent with **cubic** scaling over the sampled range (least-squares exponent
  2.997 on the five smallest theta; ratios 7.99, 7.99, 7.98, 7.96 per doubling) —
  `research/qubit_endpoint_scaling.py`.

All three are numerical observations on sampled ranges, produced by local optimizers whose
per-run status and residuals were not retained. Shipping the source permits a rerun; it does
not establish the historical execution of the runs reported here, and floating-point results
are not bitwise reproducible across environments. The cubic exponent is a fit to the
optimized envelope over that range: it is not an asymptotic law, and it does not assert that
the derivative vanishes for every fixed measurement configuration.

What this material is good for is explaining why the bound was hard to reach numerically:
the bound is *attained*, so a uniform grid with a global Lipschitz estimate cannot certify
it, and the margin near the maximizer is small over a wide neighbourhood. That is a
limitation of that particular technique, not an impossibility theorem — boundary-aware
certification and analytic treatment near equality are not excluded. An exact positive-Gram
operator identity sidesteps the issue entirely, which is what the sharp certificate does.

## Scope, and what is not established

- The certificate proves an **operator** inequality for unit Bloch vectors. With the
  projective-extreme, deterministic-observable and Schmidt-compression arguments it covers all
  Schmidt-number-two behaviors. It says nothing about Schmidt number three or higher beyond
  the separately certified `7.041387041`.
- The gate-free theorem excludes decompositions consisting *entirely* of compatible-pair
  components. It does not show that every decomposition contains an incompatible component in
  some stronger sense.
- The quantum violation is `0.0129` against a local bound of 7 that `H` does not raise, so the
  noise tolerance remains roughly `0.18%`. Nothing here is an experimental proposal.
- **Novelty is unresolved and no reviewer has audited it.** Bell-inequality dimension
  witnesses are a well-populated field, and one-sided partial-locality hulls are characterized
  in arXiv:1902.05841. The candidate contribution is the two-sided convexification and the
  combination of the two certifications in one functional — narrow, and unaudited.
- No human expert has reviewed any of this.

## Corrections, 6 September 2026

Applied by the author of this review after a maintainer review of commit `f68a6aa`. The
original text of the corrected passages is superseded, not hidden:

1. **A stated reason was wrong.** An earlier version of `research/qubit_endpoint_scaling.py` said the
   derivative vanishes at `theta = 0` because "both the c and s derivatives vanish at s = 0".
   With `c = cos(2 theta)` and `s = sin(2 theta)` the correct values are `c'(0) = 0` and
   `s'(0) = 2`. The transverse term vanishes because `s` enters squared and `s(0) = 0`, not
   because `s'(0) = 0`. The explanation has been removed rather than patched, since a
   derivative statement for a fixed configuration is in any case not a statement about the
   optimized envelope.
2. **Sample counts were overstated.** This review previously reported "about 5 million
   samples" per profile point. The shipped script performs 880,000. The figure has been
   corrected to what the code does.
3. **A claimed run was not shipped.** The 600-restart joint search was run but not included.
   It is now supplied as `research/qubit_joint_search.py` rather than left as an external report.
4. **Two claims were overreach.** The assertion that no grid-plus-Lipschitz argument can ever
   certify this bound is now scoped to that technique. The suggestion that any SOS
   certificate must vanish to third order at the maximizer — inferred from a numerical fit to
   an optimized envelope — has been withdrawn entirely; it was not a theorem.
